Flame lab: one NASA test, then your own experiment

Pick a microgravity fire test from NASA's PSI database. Its oxygen, pressure and fuel set up the flame. Then change gravity, add cabin airflow, blow on the flame, or look inside it: the chamber can show where the oxygen is, where the heat is and how the gas moves.

Gravity

NASA ran every one of these tests at 0 g. Any other value is the same air with gravity added.

Air moving across the chamber from the left, as a cabin fan would push it. A relative setting, not cm/s. Only SAFFIRE records airflow in NASA's tables, so this is your experiment, not a NASA test.

Real physics

Fuel only burns where it meets oxygen, and each bit of fuel uses up a fixed share of oxygen. Hot gas rises only when there is gravity. Gases spread faster at low pressure. A flame goes out when it loses heat faster than it makes it. These rules are in the equations.

From NASA's table

Oxygen, pressure, fuel, and whether the flame lit and went out. These come from the test's own row and are never filled in or estimated. At 0 g with no airflow, the flame follows NASA's record of the test.

Simplified, not a prediction

The chamber is flat (two dimensions), every fuel burns with the same made-up strength, and sizes, speeds and heat are in the model's own units. Use it to see directions and compare settings. Colours in the Flame view are an illustration.
